# People aren't Cargo

## Description

This is my final project for Data Visualisation and Sensing. It is about slavery in the golden age of piracy. The inspiration came from a deleted scene from Pirates of the Caribbean: At World's End. I plan on developing this project further, this is the first working version.

## Concept
It starts off with a compass surrounded by options and the question:

> "What is it you want most?"

This is a reference to the movie Pirates of the Caribbean. The compass in the film doesn't point North but rather to what the users heart wants most. The compass follows the moouse cursor and there are options to what someone may want, the majority are basic necessities and rights that almost everyone has, this was intentional as during the transatlantic slave trade, slaves were stripped of their dignity, life, family and far much more which historical records barely scratch the surface of. All of these losses are a result of their freedom being taken away. Whichever the option the user chooses, the text will change to the word "Freedom", showing how none of their desires can be obtained unless they have their freedom.

Once an option is selected, the user is redirected to the next page where they will be see the text:


> "This isn't a compass that points north, but to what the heart seeks most.\
> Not all treasure is silver and gold.\
> For some it is freedom"


This is yet another nod to the films, showing that the most valuable thing to a person is not always material posessions. During the slave trade, slaves did had no posessions. They had minimal rations, clothing, shelter and no free will. They weren't even allowed to keep their own names, completely stripping them of their dignity, humanity and life. I used this quote as a way to represent the signigicant impact of removing a persons freedom has.

Once the text has disappeared, the user will see a data visualisation. Seeing thousands of stars in a night sky.
I used stars to represent each individual slave that was transported on the ships in my dataset for two reasons. The compass in the films is seen to have a starry scene inside it. The other being the representation. Stars are all unique, there are an incomprehensible amount of stars in the universe, and barely a fraction of that is visible from earth. This represents the magnitude of slavery, how despite the great number of stars seen in the visualisation, there are countless of slaves whose fates haven't been recorded, and that my data, although vast, barely scratches the surface. On top of this, despite a stars uniqueness, they all appear indistinguishable to one another, simularly to how slaves were stripped of their individuality, during the slave trade, they were nothing but a mere tool, a number. Stars, although they dont live forever, last for thousands to millions of years, much like the impact that the slave trade has on people of colour in the world from casual racism to genocide.

This visualisation holds alot of significance to me and my heritage, and despite the lack of recordings and data, I attempted to show the scale and impace that slavery had on the countless of people who were enslaved. The visualisation represents the number of people who passed while in transit as well as the number of children who were seperated from their families.

## Features
- Represents a fragment of the scale of the transatlantic slave trade during the years 1650 to 1730, during the golden age of piracy
- Shows the number of people who boarded the slave ships
- Ability to switch the colours of the visualisation to filter by:
  -  the number of deaths -> red stars
  -  the number of children -> yellow stars
- Can pause the visualisation and scroll through the time line to see the scale and drastic increase of the number of people over time 


## Getting Started

**Link to the website:** [https://cci.arts.ac.uk/~kidubl/people-arent-cargo/](https://cci.arts.ac.uk/~kidubl/people-arent-cargo/)

### How to use:
- Click the start button, and an audio will play.
- Move the cursor around, and the compass point will follow the cursor,
- Click on one of the options that appear on screen to go to the main visualisation.
- Some text will appear on screen explaining the project,
- After some ti,me it will disappear and the visualisation will play.
- The visualisation will move automatically every 3 seconds,
- Use the slider to go through the years and the Play/Pause button to pause or resume the simulation.
- There are two buttons, one red and one yellow, which can be used to switch between viewing the number of children and the number of deaths
- The yellow stars are the children, red are the deaths, and white are the remaining embarked.
